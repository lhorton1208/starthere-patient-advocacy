What Does a Patient Advocate Actually Do?

Many people hear the term "patient advocate" but are not exactly sure what it means. Is a patient advocate a nurse? A case manager? A family helper? An insurance expert?

The answer is that a patient advocate can support patients and families in many different ways, depending on their needs.

At StartHere Patient Advocacy Services, our role is to help patients better understand, organize, and navigate the healthcare system with confidence.

A Patient Advocate Works for the Patient

An independent patient advocate is different from someone employed by a hospital, clinic, or insurance company.

A patient advocate's primary responsibility is to the patient and their family. The goal is to help the patient feel informed, supported, and heard throughout their healthcare journey.

Helping Patients Understand Medical Information

Medical language can be confusing. Patients may hear unfamiliar terms, receive complex test results, or be asked to make decisions quickly.

A patient advocate can help explain information in plain language, prepare questions for doctors, and make sure the patient understands the next steps.

Preparing for Doctor Appointments

Appointments can feel rushed, and it is easy to forget important questions.

A patient advocate can help patients prepare by organizing symptoms, concerns, medications, medical history, and questions before the visit.

After the appointment, the advocate can help review what was discussed and what actions need to be taken.

Attending Appointments

For some patients, having another person present during appointments can be extremely helpful.

A patient advocate can listen carefully, take notes, ask clarifying questions, and help make sure the patient's concerns are addressed.

Coordinating Care

Patients with complex health needs may see several providers, including primary care physicians, specialists, therapists, pharmacists, and home health services.

A patient advocate can help coordinate communication among these providers and keep track of recommendations, referrals, and follow-up care.

Supporting Hospital Visits and Discharge Planning

Hospital stays can be stressful for patients and families. Treatment plans may change quickly, and discharge instructions can be difficult to understand.

A patient advocate can help families ask the right questions, understand the care plan, and prepare for a safe transition home.

Organizing Medical Records

Medical information is often scattered across different hospitals, clinics, patient portals, and paper documents.

A patient advocate can help gather and organize records, test results, medication lists, appointment summaries, and care instructions.

Helping with Insurance and Billing Questions

Insurance approvals, denied claims, referrals, and medical bills can create major stress for families.

A patient advocate can help review documents, organize information, and support communication with insurance companies and healthcare providers.

Supporting Aging Parents and Family Caregivers

Many adult children are responsible for helping aging parents manage healthcare decisions, even when they live far away.

A patient advocate can provide support by attending appointments, coordinating care, and keeping family members informed.

Reducing Stress and Confusion

Most families do not seek a patient advocate because they want someone to "take over." They seek an advocate because they want guidance, clarity, and peace of mind.

A patient advocate helps families feel more organized and less alone.
