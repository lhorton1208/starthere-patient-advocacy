Why Every Family Should Consider a Patient Advocate

Healthcare has never been more advanced, yet it has never been more complicated. Patients and their families are often expected to make life-changing medical decisions while navigating insurance companies, hospital systems, specialists, medications, and mountains of paperwork. During these stressful moments, many people discover they need someone on their side—someone whose only priority is helping them receive the best possible care.

That's where StartHere Patient Advocacy Services comes in.

You Don't Have to Navigate Healthcare Alone

Whether it's an unexpected emergency room visit, a new cancer diagnosis, a complex surgery, or simply trying to care for an aging parent, the healthcare system can quickly become overwhelming.

Most families ask questions like:

  • What exactly did the doctor say?
  • Is this treatment really necessary?
  • Why was my insurance claim denied?
  • What medications am I supposed to take?
  • How do I schedule follow-up appointments?
  • Who is coordinating all of my care?

Unfortunately, physicians and nurses often have limited time to answer every question in detail. Families are frequently left trying to connect the dots on their own.

A patient advocate helps bridge that gap.

What Is a Patient Advocate?

A patient advocate is a trusted professional who helps patients understand, organize, and navigate every aspect of their healthcare journey.

Unlike hospitals, insurance companies, or healthcare providers, an independent patient advocate works solely for the patient and their family.

At StartHere, our mission is simple:

Help patients receive the right care, at the right time, with confidence and peace of mind.

When Can a Patient Advocate Help?

Many people believe patient advocates are only needed during major illnesses, but advocacy can be valuable in countless situations.

During a Hospital Stay

Hospitalizations are stressful and fast-paced. Families may struggle to understand changing treatment plans or communicate with multiple specialists.

A patient advocate can:

  • Explain medical information in plain language
  • Help prepare questions for physicians
  • Coordinate communication among specialists
  • Ensure patient concerns are heard
  • Help families understand discharge instructions

After Discharge

Many hospital readmissions happen because patients don't fully understand what comes next.

A patient advocate can assist with:

  • Medication reconciliation
  • Scheduling follow-up appointments
  • Coordinating home health services
  • Organizing medical records
  • Monitoring recovery progress
  • Identifying warning signs that require medical attention

Managing Chronic Illness

Conditions such as heart disease, diabetes, COPD, cancer, dementia, and autoimmune disorders often require multiple providers.

A patient advocate helps coordinate care across specialists so nothing falls through the cracks.

Insurance and Billing Support

Insurance paperwork can be confusing and frustrating.

Patient advocates help clients:

  • Understand insurance benefits
  • Review medical bills
  • Appeal denied claims
  • Coordinate prior authorizations
  • Track referrals and approvals

Instead of spending hours on hold with insurance companies, families have someone helping them navigate the process.

Helping Adult Children Care for Aging Parents

Many adults live hours—or even states—away from aging parents.

They worry constantly:

  • Is Mom taking her medications?
  • Did Dad understand what the cardiologist said?
  • Is someone attending appointments?
  • Are they safe at home?

A patient advocate provides peace of mind by serving as a trusted local partner who can attend appointments, communicate with providers, and keep family members informed.

Preventing Costly Mistakes

Healthcare errors can happen.

Patients often receive:

  • Duplicate tests
  • Conflicting medical advice
  • Incorrect medications
  • Missing referrals
  • Delayed follow-up care

Having another knowledgeable set of eyes reviewing information can help identify issues before they become larger problems.

Reducing Stress During Difficult Times

Perhaps the greatest benefit of a patient advocate is reducing anxiety.

When someone is sick, families should be focused on healing—not trying to decipher insurance forms or coordinate multiple healthcare providers.

A patient advocate becomes an experienced guide, helping families feel informed, organized, and empowered.
